Realms on the Rails

Realms on the Rails is a Multiplayer Roguelike video game that was developed among a group of seven students at Drury University in Unreal Engine 5. You and up to three other players band together to plunder a train with boxcars that serve as portals to other realms. Each car has its own objective and treasures galore. Using their gold and magic cards, players work their way to The Conductor to take over the train.

Each realm has a different objective to complete to unlock the door to the next realm. The realms are randomly generated every new run. The Tree realm requires players to collect enough acorns to unlock the next door. The Volcano realm pushes players to reach the top before being killed by lava or enemies. The Steampunk Dungeon realm gives players a complex maze to find minibosses that drop keys to unlock the door.

The Cards

These are the illustrations for the magic card abilities players may use in the game. When designing the cards, it was important for the illustration to show the player what it does and what realm it's from. I used Clip Studio Paint from start to finish for these designs. There is a half-tone pipeline I created to give the cards the comic style we were aiming to have in the game. I didn't get to polish these as much as I wanted to, but I am happy with the results. The goal was to stay true to the art style we wanted and keep consistency. All digitally hand drawn, painted, and designed by me.

Particles & Post Processing

I created particle effects for multiple assets throughout the game. I had never worked with Unreal Engine's particle system before, but I really enjoyed learning how it works. I spent a few hours watching online tutorials to better understand the system and how I could implement it into the game. Through that process, I created some solid effects that help immerse players in each realm.

I also created several post-processing effects based on online tutorials. The outline post-process creates lines along the edges of geometry, with the thickness of lines varying depending on distance. I also modified a physically based rendering effect from an online tutorial. Unlike traditional cel shading, which creates distinct shading steps primarily through lighting, this effect evaluates individual pieces of geometry and creates gradient steps based on the geometry's colors and textures.

Fish TD

Fish TD is a tower defense game developed by Jon-Paul Thompson and myself for a game jam. The game was developed in Godot Engine and was the first time I had worked in it. I worked on the coding, scripts, and finding free art assets. It was the first time I used AI to help create code for the game and turned out to be very useful in creating foundational logic. This was ultimately built in 24-48 hours for the most part.

The fish enemies follow similar logic to balloons in "Bloons TD 5". Based off the color of the fish, they have different health and speed. The enemies are spawned in waves, with each wave having a set number of enemies. The player must defend their base from the enemies by placing towers that shoot projectiles at them. The towers can be upgraded to increase their damage and range. By killing enemies, the player can earn sand dollars, to upgrade damage and range of the towers. The towers we added in the game are the Pistol Shrimp and the Cone Snail. The Pistol Shrimp shoots faster bullets, while the Cone Snail shoots slower but does more damage.

ICU

ICU is a 3D survival horror developed by a group of four students at Drury University. The game was developed in Unreal Engine 5 and was the first time I had worked in it. The player must find the three buttons to unlock the door to the next level. The player must avoid the monster that is chasing them and can use a flare gun to temporarily stun it. The player can view through the eye of the monster to see where it is looking, so they can avoid the monster.

The game was developed over a couple months and was a great learning experience for the first time working in Unreal Engine. I created the flare gun model and textures, as well as the flare gun projectile. I also created the flare gun's functionality and logic in Blueprints. I troubleshooted the bugs and issues during development and merged everyone's work into the main branch of the project. The mechanic of viewing through the eye of the monster was borrowed from my previous project, Headloose.

Dark Web

Dark Web is a text adventure and was the first game I created. The player must navigate through their apartment building to escape from the online terrorist group. Their sanity (health) dwindles at the thought of what the group is capable of. The player must make choices that will affect their sanity and one of the five outcomes of the game. The player can go upstairs, ground floor, and the basement. To escape, the player must find the key to unlock the door to the main entrance of the building. This game won first place out of the 14 games that were submitted for the class.
